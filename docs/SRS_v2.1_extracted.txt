Lufthansa-Inspired Airline Management & Booking System
Version 2.1 — Full Customer Platform + Full Admin Dashboard (Reviewed & Corrected)
Academic / Software Engineering Project
1. Introduction
1.1 Purpose
This Software Requirements Specification defines the functional and non-functional requirements for a Lufthansa-inspired airline booking and management platform. The system combines a customer-facing travel platform with a full administrative dashboard through which authorized administrators can manage the major business operations of the platform.
1.2 Project Scope
The system shall support flight discovery and booking, passenger management, payments, seats, baggage, online check-in, flight status, notifications, loyalty services, and booking management. The administrative dashboard shall provide centralized management of flights, routes, airports, aircraft, users, bookings, payments, baggage, loyalty, notifications, reports, settings, staff, roles, permissions, and audit logs.
1.3 Important Project Boundary
This is a Lufthansa-inspired educational system. It is not an official Lufthansa system and shall not use private Lufthansa operational systems or claim to provide real airline inventory. Live operational data may be mocked or integrated through authorized external services.
2. System Objectives
Provide a complete and user-friendly airline booking experience.
Provide administrators with centralized control over the system.
Maintain consistent flight, booking, passenger, payment, and seat data.
Support secure authentication and role-based authorization.
Provide analytics and reports for operational decision-making.
Record important administrative and business actions through audit logs.
Provide an architecture that can scale as users, flights, and bookings increase.

8. Business Rules
BR-01: A confirmed booking must reference at least one valid passenger and one valid flight segment.
BR-02: A seat cannot be assigned to more than one passenger on the same flight segment.
BR-03: A flight cannot exceed its configured passenger capacity.
BR-04: A booking cannot become confirmed unless the required payment state is successful.
BR-05: Payment failure shall not create a confirmed booking.
BR-06: Cancellation, refund, and rebooking eligibility shall follow configured fare/business rules.
BR-07: Check-in shall only be available when the booking and flight satisfy configured eligibility rules.
BR-08: Only authorized staff may perform administrative operations.
BR-09: Super Admin shall have full administrative permissions.
BR-10: Sensitive administrative actions shall create audit-log records.
BR-11: An audit log record shall identify the actor, action, target, and timestamp.
BR-12: Customer users shall not access other customers' private booking or payment information.
BR-13 (new): The system shall place a temporary hold on a selected seat during checkout to prevent concurrent double-booking; the hold shall expire automatically if payment is not completed within a configured time window.
BR-14 (new): Administrative confirmation of a booking without a successful payment record shall only occur through an explicitly authorized exception workflow, and every such exception shall generate an audit-log entry (resolves the BR-04 / Section 5.6 conflict).
BR-15 (new): Cancellation and refund amounts shall be calculated according to configured fare rules, applicable cancellation fees, and the original payment amount.

15. Assumptions and Constraints
The project is an educational Lufthansa-inspired system, not an official Lufthansa product.
Live flight inventory, airport operational systems, payment processing, and baggage tracking may require external APIs or mocked services.
Real-world airline policies may vary by route, fare, date, and operating carrier; project rules should therefore be configurable.
Development may initially use seeded/mock flight data.
External integrations should be isolated behind services/interfaces so they can be replaced by mocks during development.
The baseline technology stack is defined in Section 19. Any replacement technology shall preserve the stated functional, security, data-integrity, and traceability requirements.
16. Requirements Traceability
Every functional requirement should later be mapped to at least one use case, UI screen, API endpoint, database entity, and test case. This provides a traceable path from Market Research → Requirements → Design → Implementation → Testing.
17. Market Research Basis
The system scope is inspired by publicly visible Lufthansa concepts including flight search and booking, customer account services, manage-booking functions, online check-in, flight status, baggage services, and loyalty-related services. The project should validate exact current Lufthansa behavior against official public documentation before presenting any specific operational rule as a real Lufthansa requirement.
18. Version 2.1 Review — Change Log (new)
The following gaps, contradictions, and omissions were identified in v2.0 and corrected in this version:
Added a System / Scheduler actor (Section 3) — automated jobs (status sync, seat-hold expiry, notifications) had no owning actor.
FR-C02 & new FR-C24 — no account-recovery (password reset) or MFA requirement existed for a system handling payment data.
FR-C04 — flight search did not specify trip type (one-way / round-trip / multi-city).
FR-C09 & BR-13 & SeatHold entity — no mechanism prevented two customers from concurrently booking the same seat during checkout (a real double-booking risk).
FR-C13 & NFR-01 — no requirement barring storage of raw card data or requiring PCI DSS–compliant payment handling.
Section 5.6 & BR-14 — admin 'Confirm booking' capability could bypass BR-04's payment-success requirement with no stated safeguard; now scoped to an audited exception workflow.
BR-15 — no rule governed how refund/cancellation amounts are actually calculated.
NFR-10 — privacy requirement did not reference data-protection regulation (e.g., GDPR) or retention/deletion periods, despite Lufthansa being EU-based.
NFR-13 — no localization requirement existed despite the platform targeting an international customer base.
NFR-01 — no anti-abuse/rate-limiting requirement for public-facing search and booking endpoints.

This section defines the implementation baseline for the requirements in this SRS. It refines the high-level architecture in Section 11 and establishes a shared contract between the Angular frontend, NestJS backend, PostgreSQL database, and Prisma data-access layer. The design is intentionally modular so that external payment, notification, and operational integrations can be mocked during development and replaced without changing the customer-facing requirements.

19.2 Logical Application Architecture
The Angular application communicates with NestJS over HTTPS using JSON APIs. NestJS owns business rules and authorization decisions; Angular must not be treated as a security boundary. Prisma is the only application data access layer for PostgreSQL, and all multi-record booking, payment, seat-hold, check-in, and cancellation updates shall use explicit transaction boundaries. Scheduled workers are responsible for status synchronization, expiry of seat holds, notification retries, and other automated actions described in the requirements.
• Angular feature areas shall be organized around search, booking, manage booking, check-in, customer profile, and administration.
• NestJS modules shall isolate flights, bookings, payments, operations, users and RBAC, notifications, reporting, and audit concerns.
• PostgreSQL constraints and indexes shall enforce uniqueness and lookup requirements such as IATA codes, booking references, payment provider references, and seat-hold expiry queries.
• Prisma migrations shall be version-controlled and applied through the project deployment process; production schema changes shall not be made manually.

19.5 Prisma and PostgreSQL Design Rules
• Every persistent entity shall have a stable UUID primary key unless a documented external identifier is required for interoperability.
• Natural business identifiers such as booking reference, IATA code, member number, tag number, and provider reference shall have explicit unique constraints where applicable.
• Foreign keys shall use restrictive or cascading behavior deliberately. Booking and payment records shall be retained for audit and financial traceability rather than deleted casually.
• Seat allocation shall be protected by a combination of active seat holds, transaction isolation, and database uniqueness rules appropriate to the booking lifecycle.
• Sensitive values shall be minimized, encrypted or tokenized where required, excluded from logs, and subject to the retention and deletion requirements in the SRS.
• Prisma schema names may follow project TypeScript conventions while PostgreSQL table and column mappings remain explicit through @@map and @map where a stable SQL naming convention is needed.
19.6 Traceability and Implementation Acceptance Criteria
A feature is considered technically traceable when its requirement identifier can be followed through a use case, Angular screen or workflow, NestJS endpoint and service, Prisma model or transaction, PostgreSQL constraint or index where relevant, and automated test case. This traceability path extends the requirement statement in Section 16 and shall be maintained as the implementation evolves.
• The repository shall contain the Prisma schema and migration history for every released database change.
• API DTOs, authorization guards, and service methods shall be covered by unit or integration tests for the corresponding business rules.
• Critical booking, seat-hold, payment, cancellation, and check-in flows shall have end-to-end tests against a PostgreSQL test database.
• The Angular and NestJS module names shall remain aligned with the mapping schema unless the traceability section is updated in the same change.
